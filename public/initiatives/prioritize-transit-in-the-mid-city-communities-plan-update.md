# Information

The first draft of the Mid-City Communities Plan Update supports transit priority measures, but many of the recommendations are framed around studying or supporting future improvements.

Let's tell the working group that a fully realized Mid-City Communities Plan is critical for the wellbeing of its residents!

# Content

Dear Mid-City CPU Working Group Members,

I’m writing to urge the CPU working group to make transit priority a network commitment, as Mid-City has been historically underserved by transit despite having the highest population density of any neighborhood in San Diego. with the greatest population density. urgent action to improve MTS Route 215—San Diego’s highest-ridership bus line and the closest thing we have to true rapid transit.

The current draft recognizes that transit reliability is affected by congestion on El Cajon Boulevard, University Avenue, Euclid Avenue, and Fairmount Avenue. It supports transit priority measures and identifies opportunities for transit lanes, peak-period transit lanes, shared bus-bike lanes, and a potential center-running bus lane on El Cajon Boulevard.

Unfortunately, many of these recommendations are framed around studying, supporting, or coordinating future improvements. The final Community Plan should implement the Mobility Master Plan's direction to provide transit priority in roadway corridors and at intersections. For example, the Rapid 215 is the transit lifeline for Mid-City, linking its residents with jobs, schools, healthcare providers, and economic opportunities. Despite initial promises, the rapid route takes closer to 50 minutes rather than the advertised 30 minutes, due to key features that are incomplete or inconsistently implemented. Committing to prioritizing improvements, such as extending the continuous, red-painted bus-only lanes from Mid-City to SDSU and upgrading the transit signal priority system, are relatively low-cost, high-impact investments that would dramatically improve travel times and reliability for tens of thousands of daily riders.

We urge you to establish El Cajon Boulevard and University Avenue as priority transit corridors, with specific commitments to transit signal priority, queue jumps, dedicated or peak-period transit lanes, and other measures necessary to improve travel times and reliability. These are practical, proven upgrades that would immediately improve reliability and speed while advancing San Diego’s climate goals, equity commitments, and Vision Zero priorities.

A fully realized Mid-City Master Plan that fully fleshes out the what, where, and with what funds of critical infrastructure, transit, and housing, is extremely crucial to the vitality of the area and the prosperity of its residents for decades to come.
